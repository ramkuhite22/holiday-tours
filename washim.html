An Overview of Washim

Washim is a city in the Washim District of the state of Maharashtra. It is an old city with a rich cultural heritage. The place was called as Vatsalgulma and was ruled by the Vakataka Dynasty and some of their influences can be seen in the layout.

There are a few places that one can visit in the city and some of the interesting places are the Chamundi Devi, Padmatiritha, Shivaji Chowk, Balaji Mandir and the Antariksha Parshwanath Jain Mandir. The Shivaji Chowk is an old market and has been around for many years. The architecture of some of the buildings is also unique and one can see different influences in the city of Washim. The Balaji Temple is an old building and was built in the 18th century. One can see many devotees in this place for most parts of the year.

The Shirpur Jain Temple is one of the most important centers for pilgrimage in the area. Many Jain devotees can be seen in the temple. During the months of October and November, one can see an increased number of devotees, as this is the festival time.

Other places that are located near the city are Shegaon, Lonar and Chilkaldara and a few other towns.

Tourist Attraction in Washim

Chamunda Devi Temple

The Chamunda Devi Temple is situated in the district is quite famous among the devotees. Large number of devotees come every year in this temple.

Padmateerth

Washim is Known to have 108 tirthas, holy places or sacred springs, associated with different gods and rishis. The Padmatirtha is one of the chief Tirthas erected by Vishnu. This tirtha is basically is the temple of "Lord Shiva". This is one of the ancient temple in Washim. It is situated near a pond. The ambience of the temple is quite peaceful and spiritual.

Pohara Devi Temple

This is also the ancient temple in the district. It is situated in the Taluka Manora.

Shri Nath Nange Maharaj Temple

This is the temple in Dawha Taluka Malegaon in the Washim district. It is made of white marbal. The sculpture of the temple are quite mesmerising.

Shri Nrusimha Saraswati Swami Maharaj(Guru Mandir)

Shri Nrusimha Saraswati Swami Maharaj is the 2nd avatar (incarnation) of Lord Dattatreya. Born in Karanja, He travelled far and wide in what are now known as the States of Maharashtra, Karnataka, and Andhra Pradesh. He achieved Sainthood and performed several miracles. He imparted spiritual knowledge to his disciples, many of whom themselves attained Sainthood.

Shri Sakharam Maharaj Temple

This is the temple of Maharaj Sakharam in the washim district. It is situated Loni Taluka Risod in the district. This is the historic temple of Hindu religion.

Deo talav

The Deo talav also known as Balaji talav a large square tank with stone built sides, strongly and handsomely finished and with a jalakridasthana, resting place for swimmers in the middle, was laid out at the time of the construction of Balaji mandir in 1770 A D. The temple is flanked on one side by the temple of Vyankateshvar Balaji and on the oilier by that of Ramchandra.

The tree, plantations by tile side of the tank have now thoroughly disappeared. During the Ganapati festival, the immersion of the idols takes place in this tank and as a result this tank is getting silted gradually. However, the tank still stands in good condition.

Daridrya Harana Tirtha

The Daridrya-harana Tirtha is said to have been created by Shri Dattatreya. Well built as the tank seems t0 have been formely, the steps on only one side are noticeable now. By the side of the tank is a large banyan tree. One anecdote about the tirtha says that king Dashratha of Ayodhya, the father of Rama, killed Shravana by mistake by sitting over this tree.

Rama Mandir

On the other side of the Deo talav is a temple dedicated to Ramchandra, a large enclosed building but not, by any means as fine as the temple of Baiaji. it contains images of Lakshmana, Sita, Maruti and Rariha Krishna besides that of Ramchandra. It is said to have been built by one Bhagvandas Maharaj Bairagi about 250 years ago. In front of the temple, has recently been constructed a two storeyed dharmashala. It is used by the bairagis who visit the temple. Marriage and such other religious functions also take place in this dharmashala. Ramanavami is celebrated at this temple with great pomp.

Madhyameshvar Mandir

This temple was constructed about 5 to 7 years ago. After entering a big audience hall there is an inner chamber where is placed a shalunka of Shiva. At the time of the construction of the temple some images and inscriptions were excavated at the site. The temple is said to have been constructed at a place from where according to the belief of astronomers passes the equador and hence the temple is known as the Madhyameshvara temple.

How to Reach

Since Washim is an important center for pilgrimage, there are regular bus services from different parts of the state. The place is well connected by road and can be accessed easily.

Air

The closest airport is located at Aurangabad and there are a few flights that operate from the airport. The nearest major airport is located at Mumbai and taxis are available from both the places. They charge around Rs 7 per km for non A/C sedans. SUVs are also available.

Airports near Washim by Type and Aerial Distance

  • Nanded Airport (NDC) Domestic 106 km
  • Chikkalthana Airport, Aurangabad (IXU) Domestic 195 km
  • Rajiv Gandhi International Airport, Hyderabad (HYD) International 351 km
  • Lohegaon Airport, Pune (PNQ) Domestic 396 km

Train

The city is well connected by its railway station and there are frequent trains that arrive from different parts of the state. The station is connected to cities such as Jaipur and Mumbai. Taxis and autos are available from outside the station. Auto rickshaws are the best way to reach the city center and prices are negotiable

Bus

Buses are available from Mumbai to the city of Washim. Both government run buses and private buses are available. Private buses are more comfortable and the distance is around 580 km and the travel time is around 10 hours. Private buses cost around Rs 400 for the trip and these are a comfortable option. Government buses cost around Rs 100 and are generally uncomfortable for travelling long distances.
